Overview

Perpside is a launchpad for coins backed by real assets — with no bonding curve and no graduation event. Platforms that use a bonding curve keep a coin in a holding pattern until it crosses some volume threshold, then migrate it to a real exchange — a separate, risky step where liquidity briefly moves and prices can gap. Perpside skips that entirely: when you launch a coin, Perpside creates real, on‑chain Raydium liquidity pools for it in the same transaction flow, live from the first block. There's nothing to graduate into, because the coin launches directly into the real thing.

You choose one to three backing assets for a coin — the built‑in Hylo leveraged tokens (xSOL, xBTC, xHYPE) or any token on Solana — and each one gets its own pool. Backing a coin with more than one asset isn't just about reaching more buyers: the pools stay linked through the coin's own price, so if two backing assets drift against each other in the wider market (which xSOL and xBTC constantly do, being leveraged), the coin's implied exchange rate between them drifts out of line too. That gap is a standing arbitrage opportunity, and arbitrageurs close it by trading through the coin itself — real swaps, real fees, generated purely by keeping the coin's own markets consistent with each other. A multi‑asset coin picks up a background hum of trading activity this way, on top of whatever organic demand it earns on its own, and every one of those trades feeds the same reward model below.

Every coin is a normal SPL token the moment it launches, with its mint authority renounced and its metadata locked immutable at creation — supply is fixed forever and its name, ticker, and logo can never be changed by anyone, including Perpside. Its starting liquidity is locked too: each pool's position is locked through Raydium's own Lock CL Position program right after it's created, so that position can never be withdrawn by anyone — not the creator, not Perpside — while trading fees it earns can still be collected and routed into the reward model. It can be bought, sold, and held anywhere Solana tokens are supported.

Launching a Coin

Launching takes one form, filled out in a few steps:

  1. Identity — upload an image, and set a name and ticker.
  2. Backing assets — pick 1–3 assets from xSOL, xBTC, and xHYPE, or search any Solana token by name, symbol, or mint address and add it to the mix.
  3. Reward model — turn on Community, Creator, Buyback & Burn, or any combination, and set each one's fee within its cap.
  4. First buy (optional) — set an amount of SOL to buy immediately at launch, capped at 10% of total supply so a creator can't corner their own coin at launch.
  5. Links (optional) — attach an X account, Telegram, and website so traders can find the community.

Submitting charges one fee, paid straight to the transaction — sized to the real on‑chain cost of minting the coin and creating its pool(s), plus a small margin. It isn't a flat platform markup, and it exists mainly as anti‑spam: launching costs the same whether Perpside sponsors it or not, so this just makes sure the person launching is the one paying for what they're creating.

Once that transaction lands, everything else — the mint, the metadata, every pool and its starting liquidity — is created in the same flow. There's no waiting period and no second step: the coin appears in Explore already tradeable, because it already is.

Reward Model & Fees

Launching sets the pool's actual on‑chain trading fee, then splits 100% of it across up to three destinations. Both steps are configured at launch:

  • Trading fee — picked from Raydium's own published fee tiers, not a free‑form number. Raydium only lets pools charge one of its pre‑approved rates (creating a custom rate isn't possible — that's admin‑only on Raydium's program), so the launch form offers exactly the tiers that exist on whichever network the coin launches on.
  • Community — the share of that fee paid directly to every holder's wallet, in proportion to their balance.
  • Creator — the share paid to the coin's creator.
  • Buyback & Burn — the share used to buy the coin back from the market and burn it, permanently reducing supply.

At least one of Community / Creator / Buyback & Burn must be enabled, and whichever are enabled always split 100% of the chosen trading fee between them — e.g. a 1% fee split 33% / 33% / 34% pays 0.33% to holders, 0.33% to the creator, and 0.34% to buyback & burn, out of every trade. Enabling or disabling a destination re‑splits evenly across whatever's left on; each slider can then be adjusted from there.

Collection runs automatically: every couple of hours, a background process checks each coin's accrued trading fees, and once they cross a threshold worth collecting, harvests them and pays out the configured split — Community sent to every holder in proportion to their balance, Creator sent directly to the coin's creator, Buyback & Burn swapped into the coin and burned. Very small per-holder shares are skipped rather than spent on a transfer that would cost more than it's worth. Perpside is beta software; verify a coin's actual payouts on-chain rather than assuming the configured split alone.

Trading & Wallet

The Explore page lists every coin launched on Perpside, sorted by Trending, New, or Top market cap, with search by name or ticker. Clicking a coin opens its live pool on Jupiter — the same pool Perpside created at launch — where you can trade it directly against its backing asset.

Trading happens through your own Solana wallet — Phantom, Jupiter, Backpack, Solflare, and others are detected automatically when you connect. Perpside never takes custody of your funds and never has access to your keys; every trade is a transaction you sign yourself, straight against the pool, on‑chain.

Risk & Disclaimers

Coins backed by xSOL, xBTC, or xHYPE carry leveraged exposure to their underlying asset — their price can move sharply in either direction, faster than the underlying asset itself. Coins backed by other tokens carry that token's own volatility. Arbitrage between a coin's pools also means its price can move on days when nothing about the coin itself changed — only its backing assets did.

Perpside's own wallet still creates every coin's mint and every pool's starting liquidity position — that part of launching isn't decentralized. What it can't do is take that liquidity back afterward: mint authority is renounced, metadata is immutable, and each pool's position is locked through Raydium's own Lock CL Position program right after it opens, closing off the only path Raydium's program allows for withdrawing it — while still allowing accrued trading fees to be collected separately. Weigh the part that's still centralized (who creates a coin) separately from the part that's provably locked (what happens to it once created).

Perpside is beta software. Contracts, fee mechanics, and supported assets can change, and bugs are possible despite testing. Only trade or launch with funds you're prepared to lose, and always verify a coin's backing assets and reward model before trading it.
